Research / Digital Holographic Microscopy
Digital Imaging & Optical Computing · 2007

Digital Holographic Microscopy

Published algorithms for reconstructing digital in-line holograms, detecting biological micro-organisms and extending the reconstruction process toward three-dimensional target tracking.

Public record ↗Publications
Problem

Recover useful three-dimensional information from recorded in-line holograms.

Digital in-line holographic microscopy records three-dimensional optical information in a hologram, but useful biological interpretation requires numerical reconstruction, detection and tracking across depth.

Contribution

Reconstruction, organism analysis and depth tracking in one computational workflow.

The work develops a reconstruction algorithm for biological organisms such as Daphnia, demonstrates anatomy identification, and proposes a quasi-automatic algorithm for tracking moving objects in depth.

Method & Evidence

From holographic video to reconstructed 3D motion

The published workflow decomposes recorded holographic video into frames, reconstructs each frame at multiple depths, and uses image-processing operations to identify the most likely object depth over time.

Reconstruction

Digital wave-front recovery

Recorded in-line holograms are numerically reconstructed into image planes at selected depths.

Biological demonstration

Daphnia

The paper demonstrates reconstruction of a biological organism and identification of part of its anatomy.

Tracking

3D target trajectory

A quasi-automatic method is proposed to follow moving targets through reconstructed depth planes over time.
